## Verdict

The confirmatory analysis, its framing and the reproducibility evidence hold up. Apart from one stale count (M4), every number in the paper matches its table. There is one go/no-go issue for the author (C1) and five things to fix before anyone else reviews the paper (M1-M5).

## Critical: decisions for the author

**C1. TMLR's LLM policy.** TMLR's FAQ (read 17 Nov) allows LLMs as assistive tools, requires a first-page footnote, and states: "our expectation is that the ideas, claims and results found in submissions are human-sourced, not produced by such tools." Our footnote says an LLM was used "to design and implement the analyses and to draft text". That is accurate, and it sits close to what the expectation rules out. The archived plan also says it was "approved under authority the researcher delegated to the analysis assistant". The paper also credits its two counterexamples and a recomputation to "independent reviews"; those reviews came from a second LLM. Options, which only the author can choose between:
(a) Before submitting, ask the TMLR Editors-in-Chief whether this division of work is acceptable, describing it exactly. (Recommended.)
(b) Make sure the author has read, understood and can defend every design choice, and rewrite the footnote to state the division precisely: the question and constraints came from the author; design, code and drafting from LLM assistants; review by a second LLM; all checked by the author.
(c) Choose a venue whose policy explicitly accepts AI-conducted research.
Wording that understates the AI's role is not an option.

**C2. "The authors reviewed all content."** This is not yet true. It becomes true only after the author's read-through.

## Major: fix before external review

**M1. Burtch (2026) is misdescribed.** The bibliography uses the v1 title; the current version is v3 (29 Sep 2026), titled "...: AI Homogenizes Syntax, Not (Necessarily) Semantics". The "158 contests" figure comes from v2. v3's overfitting analysis uses 129 competitions and drops teams that posted a public kernel. Neither v2 (main text) nor v3 says that the best-public-submission shift is biased by selection; that is our own observation, attributed to them. Fix: cite v3, describe v3, and state the selection point as ours. Our own literature note had marked this paper "remain to be reviewed".

**M2. The plan's deviations are not listed in the paper.** Plan §11 promises that they will be. The logged deviations are: the accuracy test also requires scores in [0,1]; the 1,000 rule was applied to finite scores, removing 2 competitions; bootstrap with competitions sorted by identifier; the label audit was done by a second reader who was not blind to labels or results; exact score reading changed four original-sample rows that the paper does not use. Fix: add a short "Deviations from the plan" paragraph.

**M3. Pre-specified secondary results are only partly reported.** The paper omits:
- S1 within E: regression relative drift 2.1% against classification 0.79%; difference 1.3 points (-1.2, 8.2).
- S2 by cohort.
- S3: drift by host category, and the code-only drift difference (4.3% against 1.2%, p = 0.03).
- S5: the share of all submissions with p <= 0.05 (O 3.6%, E 2.2%).
Reviewers of a pre-registered study look for exactly this. Fix: one appendix table listing every S1-S6 quantity, with a pointer from Section 6.

**M4. Wrong count.** "Only two accuracy competitions in E ended after 2022." The analysed E sample has six: 14036, 24105, and four Playground competitions with numeric-ID "Accuracy Score" metrics (68479, 73290, 84895, 91718). The "two" was copied from plan §10, which was written before numeric-ID metrics were mapped by name; the plan's own §4 maps them.

**M5. Overstated composition claim.** "Within E, notebook-only competitions in these categories do not differ clearly from the others, so the platform contrast above is mostly one of composition." Within Featured/Research, the median relative gap is 0.30 code-only against 0.057 not (Mann-Whitney p = 0.12; unflagged only p = 0.027). The other categories' median is about 0.005-0.007. On a log scale, composition and the within-category difference each account for roughly half of the contrast. Fix: report both numbers and drop "mostly".
