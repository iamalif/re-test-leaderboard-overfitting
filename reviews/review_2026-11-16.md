# Independent submission review — 16 November 2026

**Recommendation: major revision before submission. Continue the research, but do not submit this draft as it stands.**

The descriptive re-test and the Allstate reconstruction have value. The central claim that adaptation is “real, systematic and small” is not established by the current design. I found a nonadaptive counterexample that survives all four robustness checks, a sample-size identification problem, and a dependence problem in the selection adjustment. These are substantive gaps between evidence and claims, not cosmetic requests. They can be addressed without contacting the original authors. The defensible route is a carefully scoped empirical re-test plus an account of what leaderboard scores can and cannot identify.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Major: positive drift does not identify adaptation, even after all four checks

**Type: demonstrated identification failure and overclaim.** Evidence: `paper/main.tex:21`, `:68`, `:141`, `:156`; `scripts/stage4_drift_robustness.py:37–42`; `scripts/stage2_pilot_original.py:60–74`.

Subtracting early and late gaps cancels a *constant additive score gap*. It does not cancel fixed differences in the composition or difficulty of two test sets when those differences interact with the models being submitted. “Fixed test sets” and “a fixed score gap for every model” are different assumptions. The abstract says the measure is unaffected by fixed differences between sets, which is too strong.

The score-level control is a pooled linear within-team regression, not a general control for changing model quality. Both its outcome and its score-level covariate are functions of the same two noisy scores. It cannot rule out nonlinear learning curves, heterogeneous team slopes, or different improvements across domains. Dropping early submissions and comparing late quarters do not rule these out either. A negative initial gap does not require subsequent nonadaptive drift to be negative: learning curves can cross.

**Computed counterexample.** `outputs/review/review_checks.py` constructs 100 teams, each making 20 predetermined submissions. Nothing uses public or private feedback. For position (t\in[0,1]), let average accuracy be (b+0.1t+0.1t^2), and public-minus-private accuracy be (-0.001+0.002t). Both accuracies increase monotonically. These trajectories can be realized by nested predictions on two fixed grids of one million examples; the script rounds scores to that grid. Team baselines vary. The same project drift function and the stage-4 regression calculation give:

| Check | Result without adaptation |
|---|---:|
| Planned later-half minus earlier-half drift | +0.1053 percentage points |
| Relative drift | **1.074% of team spread** |
| Fourth minus third quarter | +0.0526 percentage points |
| Drop first two submissions | +0.0947 percentage points |
| Position coefficient controlling linearly for score level | +0.2000 percentage points |
| Mean first-submission gap | **−0.1000 percentage points** |

All four checks pass. The relative magnitude is comparable to the paper's approximately 1% headline. This is a falsification of identification, **not evidence that this specific mechanism explains Kaggle**, and not a fitted simulation of the observed competitions. Output: `nonadaptive_counterexample.csv`.

The pilot is useful as a positive control for its particular feedback mechanism, but too friendly as validation against distribution differences: its private disadvantage is an additive probability penalty, and it does not challenge the measure with different model-quality response curves. The plan actually acknowledges this alternative in section 10; the paper's conclusion and stage-4 “Neither accounts for the result” discard that caution without sufficient evidence.

**Fix:** retain the finding as a *within-team temporal public–private score divergence*. Replace the causal assertion in the abstract, methods, and conclusion with “consistent with adaptation, but not distinguishable from model-dependent differences between the two evaluation sets.” State the assumptions under which drift would identify adaptation. Include adversarial nonadaptive simulations, including this counterexample. If retaining a causal claim, obtain additional identifying information—e.g. fixed prediction matrices and verified random splits, or a genuinely exogenous feedback comparison. More regressions on the same aggregate scores cannot by themselves solve non-identifiability. Also distinguish “the analysis does not choose the best submission” from “the observed submission process is free of feedback-dependent selection or stopping.”

### 2. Major: the smallest compatible denominator is not an identified test-set size

**Type: mathematical identification error in the interpretation; the arithmetic compatibility check itself is useful.** Evidence: `scripts/stage2_measures.py:71–89`; `paper/main.tex:21`, `:34`, `:70`, `:123`, `:156`.

Under exact ordinary accuracy, if the reduced score fractions have least common denominator (d), the actual count must be a multiple of (d). Scores alone do not determine the multiplier. “Assume unweighted accuracy” does not solve this: all observed numbers of correct predictions may share a factor, including with duplicated examples or models changing predictions in groups. Floating-point tolerances, a 5,000,000 cap, and a minimum of 20 distinct scores add implementation assumptions that should also be documented. The implementation uses capped rational approximations and an all-scores lattice-fit check; it is not a proof of the uniquely correct count.

**Computed demonstration:** the 41 scores 0.40, 0.41, …, 0.80 yield denominator 100 and also fit 10,000 perfectly. For public/private accuracies 0.75/0.65, the supplied exact routine gives (p=0.16464) at 100/100 examples and (p=8.55\times10^{-54}) at 10,000/10,000. Same scores, radically different inference (`identification_examples.json`).

An exploratory sensitivity multiplying every inferred public/private size by two changes the median first-per-team rejection fraction from **1.71% to 11.12% in O**, and **1.79% to 11.75% in E**. Multiplying by ten gives 51.83% and 50.83%. These blanket multipliers are an identification stress test, **not a proposed correction**: counts supported by external evidence, especially Allstate, should not be arbitrarily multiplied. The point is that S5's reassurance depends materially on assuming the minimum is the true size. See `raw_drift_and_size_sensitivity.csv`.

**The requested 11 cases.** I independently checked the published-size compatibility against the retained submission extract. Each of the 11 pairs fails on at least one side; the discrepancy is real. Full fit fractions are in `original_11_size_disagreements.csv`.

**Fix:** call these “minimum compatible denominators.” Create an evidence-tier table: independently corroborated scoring units/split; corroborated total plus compatible split; lattice-only. Use source-supported counts for the primary formal interpretation, and show lattice-only computations conditionally with multiplier/rounding sensitivity. Revise the size-based conclusions accordingly. Do not silently replace the archived analysis: retain it, add explicitly post-plan sensitivity results, and explain what has changed in interpretation.

### 3. Major: the independent-resplitting adjustment is not a general estimate of selection bias

**Type: model misspecification relative to the claimed interpretation.** Evidence: `scripts/stage2_measures.py:135–149`; `paper/main.tex:72`, `:123`, `:143`, `:156`.

The marginal hypergeometric distribution for one fixed submission does not identify the joint distribution needed to select the top decile across submissions. The implementation resplits each submission independently. Real models share examples, often predictions, and one public/private partition. Cross-model error dependence changes the expected selection gap. A one-sentence limitation does not justify calling the residual “the part not explained by selection” or declaring two original outliers explained by selection alone.

**Computed extreme case:** 1,000 identical prediction vectors, each 70% correct on 2,000 examples, split 1,000/1,000. Under one shared random partition every submission has the same public score, all ties enter the top decile, and the expected selected gap is exactly zero. The project's independent-resplitting routine instead predicts **+3.514 percentage points** (200 repetitions, seed 20261116). It invents selection opportunities among identical models. See `identification_examples.json`. The example proves a dependence problem; it does not estimate the error in each observed contest.

**Fix:** rename this an “independent-resplitting reference adjustment” and remove claims that it isolates excess overfitting. If prediction matrices cannot be obtained, present sensitivity across dependence structures, including identical/clustered model errors, and explicitly state what aggregate scores cannot identify. Deduplicating score pairs alone cannot recover prediction dependence. Reframe the two-outlier result as compatibility with one reference model, not a mechanism established by the data. The first-per-team marginal test does not need independent draws across models to define an individual tail probability, but interpreting the distribution of those probabilities or their excess requires the dependence and split assumptions.

### 4. Moderate: clipping is missing from the paper and materially affects drift

**Type: reporting omission with measured sensitivity, not evidence of a hidden post-plan choice.** Evidence: plan section 3; `scripts/stage2_measures.py:18–24`; `scripts/stage3_extension.py:74–77`; paper methods.

For every metric except accuracy, AUC, and MAP@K, both score columns are clipped to the 1st–99th percentiles of the **public** scores. This is prespecified, but absent from the paper. It is part of the estimand: public-derived clipping is not symmetric under exchanging the two sets and can make a constant raw gap vary with model score. Robustness to pathological sentinels is a legitimate motivation; readers still need to know what the reported gaps measure.

**Recomputed:** without clipping, positive E drift is **307/481 = 63.8%**, versus 327/481 = 68.0% in the planned analysis. Fifty E competitions change sign: 35 from planned-positive to raw-negative, 15 the reverse. O changes from 77/114 to 76/114. The general positive pattern survives, so this is not a refutation of the descriptive finding. Raw values may themselves contain invalid sentinels and are not automatically preferable. The recorded exclusion for ≥99% identical scores is evaluated after clipping; I found **no additional exclusion caused solely by clipping** in these data.

**Fix:** disclose clipping in the main methods and captions as appropriate. Include the raw sensitivity with its sentinel caveat, plus a domain-valid score-cleaning or symmetric-bound sensitivity. Keep these exploratory. Do not imply that rank/sign summaries make the upstream computation of each competition's mean robust to arbitrary extreme submissions.

### 5. Moderate: the promised full-description audit was not performed as specified

**Type: documented protocol deviation and overstatement of audit coverage.** Evidence: plan section 8 promises the *full descriptions* of 50 unflagged competitions will be re-read; `scripts/stage5_label_audit_sample.py:15–16` truncates each matching sentence at 170 characters and the combined excerpt at 800; `outputs/stage5/label_audit_result.md` records the truncated procedure and same reader.

Zero changes in these excerpts is not validation against full-text missed labels or evidence that the true nonrandom-split miss rate is below 6%. The report's rule-of-three bound concerns a reliable detection process; this one has unknown sensitivity and shares the first reader's errors. This is particularly material because the log explicitly notes **12598**, a known nonrandom-split original outlier, is UNSTATED under the description-only rubric. The restriction to unflagged contests therefore does not remove the confounder in finding 1. Only two E candidates are explicitly labelled RANDOM.

**Fix:** accurately report this as a same-reader, truncated-excerpt consistency check; log the deviation. Perform the promised full-text review, ideally with a second reader, preserving the archived labels and reporting any revised analysis separately. Remove the 6% substantive miss-rate claim. The paper already says unflagged is not known random; strengthen this to explain that the sensitivity analysis controls only *documented* differences and cannot validate causal interpretation. No claim of dishonesty or score-driven relabelling is supported by this audit.

### 6. Moderate: several conclusions exceed the estimands or the tested hypotheses

**Type: interpretation and scope, not incorrect numerical tests.** Evidence: `paper/main.tex:99`, `:123`, `:145`, `:160`; `outputs/stage3_report.md` H3; `scripts/stage3_tests.py:30–54`.

- **H1:** the bootstrap intervals support small differences in the *median signed competition mean gap*, under the sampling assumptions. They do not establish that whole distributions, tails, absolute discrepancies, or generalization quality are unchanged. Signed gaps can cancel. “Public and private scores agree as closely” and “the level … has not changed” should be narrowed. Report the preset median-shift comparison explicitly. The marginal 95% intervals are not Holm-adjusted simultaneous intervals; the corrected p-values test distributional differences, not equivalence.
- **H3:** no equivalence margin was fixed for relative drift. A nonsignificant Mann–Whitney test is not evidence of unchanged magnitude. Its interval is roughly −0.33 to +0.99 percentage points of team spread, substantial relative to an original median of 0.66%. The paper's main-results “no detectable difference” is appropriate; the abstract's “same size” and report's “did not change” are not.
- **S2:** correlation with size does not establish a 10,000-example threshold, and (p=0.20) does not show the adjusted gap is independent of size. Size identification and the reference null compound the problem. Retain the observed association; remove the stronger guarantee-like interpretation.
- **Prize contests:** the exploratory label, normalization caveat, and open-mechanism statement are appropriate. However, a gap at first submission plus no detected change in drift cannot select competition design over adaptation inherited from public notebooks; the paper itself recognizes pre-first-submission adaptation. Say the observations are compatible with multiple mechanisms. Featured/Research is a category proxy, not a verified positive-prize indicator; define the shorthand or use the categories' names.
- **Composition:** 82/95 E accuracy contests are Community, but AUC is **29/73 Community**, not mostly in-class by itself. Do not extend the accuracy limitation inaccurately to AUC. “Like for like” means same metric here, not matched tasks, hosts, years, or domains. Add stratified/matched descriptive comparisons where support permits; avoid extrapolating accuracy results to modern prize contests.

**Fix:** revise claims to name the measured quantity and population; distinguish absence of detectable change from bounded median differences and from equivalence. Show tail/absolute-gap summaries and composition sensitivity as exploratory additions. No need to alter the four archived hypotheses retroactively.

## Publication assessment and concrete revision route

**Worth pursuing: yes. Ready for TMLR: no.** I would not assign a numerical acceptance probability. The present causal and selection-adjustment claims create a material soundness risk. TMLR's [acceptance criteria](https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html), checked 16 November 2026, explicitly prioritize evidence supporting claims and allow reducing claims to address gaps; they also recognize useful robustness/reproducibility studies. A new causal measure is not necessary to justify this project, but accurate interpretation is.

Recommended order:

1. Preserve the archived plan and reported planned results. Add a dated response-to-review and amended interpretation; all new analyses remain exploratory.
2. Resolve findings 1–3 in the abstract and methods before adding more observational regressions. Present temporal divergence descriptively; distinguish minimum denominators from source-verified counts; qualify or demote the independent-resplitting residual.
3. Add the denominator evidence tiers and conditional-size sensitivities; retain Allstate as the clearest source-anchored reconstruction. Do not hold the whole project hostage to unavailable speech/text answer keys.
4. Disclose clipping and include sensitivity. Complete a genuine full-description audit, preserving original labels; report uncertainty about split design rather than treating UNSTATED as random.
5. Tighten H1/H3 and prize-contest wording, examine composition/dependence where feasible, and provide a working clean-run entry point plus anonymous review materials.

A potentially defensible contribution is: **an independently audited re-test with stable median signed gaps in specified metric cohorts, documented temporal divergence, and explicit limits on inferring adaptation and test-set sizes from aggregate leaderboard scores.** This is a proposed framing, not a claim that it is novel or guaranteed publishable. A less dramatic but correct paper is a stronger submission than the current adaptation claim.
