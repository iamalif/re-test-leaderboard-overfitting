## One addition to finding 1

The review shows that drift fails to identify adaptation when public and private sets differ in composition. The converse holds too and is now stated in the paper: if the split is random, the two sets are exchangeable, and feedback-free submissions cannot produce a preferred sign of drift, whatever the models' learning curves. So drift tests a joint hypothesis: exchangeable sets and feedback-independent submissions.

- Simulation (`outputs/stage6/exchangeability_simulation.csv`): feedback-free trajectories whose gains differ across groups of examples give positive drift in 44.7% of 300 simulated competitions under a random split, and in 100% when the fastest-improving groups are placed in the public set.
- This does not rescue the original claim, because the split cannot be verified for most competitions, and where a random split is best supported the evidence is weak: split stated random, 3 of 5 positive; accuracy competitions whose first submissions fit the random-split null, 56 of 94 positive (59.6%, p = 0.08).
- First-submission gaps are positive in 56% of E competitions (p = 0.007). That is direct evidence that sets are not exchangeable in general, or that adaptation precedes first submissions.

## Checks of the review's numbers

Recomputed independently and confirmed: unclipped drift positive in 307 of 481 (E) and 76 of 114 (O), with 50 sign changes in E; median first-submission rejection share with doubled sizes 11.1% (O) and 11.7% (E).

On sizes, one piece of evidence the review did not use: in all 21 original competitions where the published size is compatible with the scores, it equals the minimum; none is a proper multiple. This supports the minimum as usually correct but does not identify it, and the paper says so. 7042 and 7634 are tier C: ratio matches the reported public share, multiplier unverified.
