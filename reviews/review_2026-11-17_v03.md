## Assessment

Most previous findings have been addressed. The manuscript now distinguishes a descriptive temporal divergence from identified adaptation, reports minimum-compatible sizes conditionally, removes the outcome-selected subgroup, and accurately describes the limitations of the second reading. Those are substantial improvements.

However, the new split-share analysis introduces a cohort error and again reaches beyond what its design establishes. These should be corrected before submission. Neither finding makes the observed drift disappear. The appropriate contribution remains a scoped re-test, an audited reconstruction, and a careful account of identification limits. I would continue the project, but would not yet sign off on submission readiness or predict acceptance.

## 1. Major interpretation issue: nominal 50/50 is not verified equal size

**Evidence:** `paper/main.tex:149` says that where sets are *reported* equal, the drift sign is balanced under a random split without feedback, then invokes 58/90 positive competitions. The symmetry argument requires actual equal-sized, exchangeable scored sets, a feedback-independent trajectory/selection rule, and a statistic that changes sign on swapping the sets. A reported percentage does not establish these conditions.

Of the 90 nominal-50% competitions in the unclipped Accuracy/AUC/MAP@K subset, 56 are accuracy competitions. Sixteen have unequal public/private minimum-compatible denominators. Examples:

| Competition ID | Public minimum | Private minimum |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 33120 | 9,000 | 12,000 |
| 24105 | 823 | 815 |
| 27644 | 1,748 | 1,745 |
| 12598 | 853 | 852 |

Several other differences are one example, plausibly reflecting rounding. But the first example implies a public share of 42.86% if the minima are the sizes. The previously documented nonrandom-split example 12598 is also in this subset.

These numbers are **not proof that actual sizes are unequal**: the two minima can have different unknown multipliers. Conversely, equal minima do not prove equal actual sizes. That uncertainty is exactly why the metadata filter cannot establish the mathematical premise. Sizes for the AUC/MAP@K competitions are not independently verified here either.

**Fix:** Call this the *nominal-50% subset*. Retain 58/90 and its exploratory comparison with 50% if useful, but do not describe it as a calibrated test of feedback-free random splitting. Explain the actual equal-size theorem separately from the empirical subset. Remove “near 50%” as a guarantee of symmetry in the script's introductory comments (`scripts/stage6_split_size_symmetry.py:5`): exact symmetry is not guaranteed by near equality.

Reproduced reference tests: combined 58/90, two-sided binomial p=0.00804593; later 37/61, p=0.123731; original 21/29, p=0.0241195. Their arithmetic is correct. Their causal/null interpretation remains conditional, and competition dependence remains unresolved.

## 2. Confirmed numerical scope error: the O+E table includes W

**Evidence:** `scripts/stage6_split_size_symmetry.py:24` and `:34` use the whole table for the label `O+E`. That table also contains cohort W, the earlier regression sample. The all-metric column in the manuscript (`paper/main.tex:153` onward) therefore includes 51 W competitions and describes 646 rather than 595 finite-drift O+E competitions.

The correctly restricted O+E results are:

| Reported public share | Correct O+E positive drift | Printed v0.3 value |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Under 40% | 244/357 (68%) | 263/394 (67%) |
| 40–49% | 35/45 (78%) | 36/46 (78%) |
| Exactly 50% | 89/139 (64%) | 95/151 (63%) |
| Over 50% | 36/54 (67%) | 37/55 (67%) |

**Fix:** Explicitly filter combined-cohort analyses to `Cohort.isin(['O', 'E'])`, regenerate the new exploratory output, and update the manuscript and response. Alternatively, explicitly label an intended three-cohort analysis, but do not call it O+E. Check both occurrences of the pooling logic.

The raw Accuracy/AUC/MAP@K column, including 58/90, is unaffected because W contains regression competitions. The corrected all-metric pattern is qualitatively similar. This is a real cohort-definition error, not evidence against the existence of positive drift.

## 3. Major interpretation issue: the table does not rule out unequal-size mechanisms generally

**Evidence:** `paper/main.tex:149` and the response conclude that “the unequal-size mechanism therefore does not account for the pattern.” The directional argument applies when holding the distribution of per-example improvements fixed and changing which set is smaller. These bands compare different competitions, tasks, participants, metrics and improvement trajectories.

For a fixed trajectory that improves every example except one, the earlier counterexample produces positive drift with probability 0.8 when the public share is 0.2. For a trajectory that improves only one example, a public share of 0.8 instead produces positive drift with probability 0.8. Both are feedback-free. Thus a population with different improvement distributions in different share bands can exhibit positive drift in both bands. The table does not identify or hold these distributions fixed.

This is a logical limitation, not a claim that this constructed explanation generated the real data. The table provides evidence against a particular homogeneous account, but cannot eliminate the broader class. Public-based clipping further prevents the all-metric column from being a direct swap-symmetry check.

**Fix:** Replace the exclusion claim with a descriptive statement, for example: “Positive drift also occurs among competitions with nominal half or larger public shares. This comparison does not verify equal-sized random splits or identify the source of the drift.” Keep the mean-drift analysis exploratory and state its metric/randomization assumptions. Avoid treating repeated post-review checks as confirmatory evidence.

## 4. Smaller residual overclaims

**First-submission inference:** `paper/main.tex:151` uses 56% positive first-submission gaps to infer that differences between sets or inherited adaptation “are common.” Sign imbalance alone does not establish the prevalence of either mechanism; the same expectation-versus-sign distinction applies, with clipping and related-competition dependence adding complications. Report 56% descriptively and list these as possible explanations, without estimating their prevalence.

**Zero expectation:** The methods appropriately qualify the expectation-zero statement to metrics averaging over examples (`paper/main.tex:72`). The abstract (`:21`) states it more broadly. Carry the qualification into the abstract or omit the expectation clause there. A general statement across arbitrary nonlinear metrics and public-dependent processing is not established by the additive-score argument.

**Simulation narrative:** The response treats the earlier 134/300 positive simulations (44.7%) as a warning that was wrongly dismissed as noise. Against a fair-sign reference, its two-sided binomial p-value is 0.0733. That run alone does not establish miscalibration. The exact counterexample establishes the logical error; use it as the evidence rather than retrospectively treating every deviation from 50% as diagnostic.

## Remaining work and limits of this review

Prioritize correcting the new table and interpretations, then finish the already acknowledged dependence/related-series sensitivity, matched before/after comparisons, clean end-to-end reproduction, and anonymous reproducibility package. Cluster definitions and matching choices should be documented before examining their new contrasts and explicitly called exploratory. A competition is a useful observational unit but is not automatically an independent draw.

The useful next step is to stabilize the defensible descriptive claim and complete reproducibility, rather than repeatedly adding new subsets intended to recover a causal interpretation from aggregate scores. A narrower honest paper can be valuable; acceptance and novelty have not been established by this review.

I did not run the full raw-data pipeline, independently identify actual test sizes for the nominal-50% subset, audit all host descriptions, complete the related-competition/matching analyses, or review an anonymous release. I did not repeat every earlier numerical check or perform a new literature search; no novelty claim is made. Review checks used the available local Python environment, not a clean installation of the specified Python 3.12 environment. No original author was contacted.
