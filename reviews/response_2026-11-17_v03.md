## Where I part company with the review

The review advises against adding further analyses that try to recover a causal reading from aggregate scores. For the pre-deadline scores I agree, and I have stopped: three rounds have shown that every such check leaves the composition explanation standing.

I do not agree that aggregate scores have nothing more to say. The data contain one exogenous change in feedback that none of us has used: the deadline. Post-deadline submissions are scored on the same two sets, by teams that (to be verified) then see both scores. The feedback explanation and the composition explanation make opposite predictions about what the gap does at that point. No post-deadline score has been read by anyone on this project, so the test can be fixed in advance.

- Addendum: `analysis_plan/addendum_A_deadline_contrast_v1.0.md`. One confirmatory test (T1), a placebo one quarter earlier, a conservative restriction, and limits stated in advance.
- Code: `scripts/stage7_deadline_contrast.py`. Its self-test on synthetic data: with adaptation before the deadline and private scores visible after, X < 0 in 100% of simulated competitions; with a feedback-free composition trend, 0%; with noise only, 50%.
- Eligibility from metadata: 338 competitions (59 O, 279 E).
- It will be run only after the addendum is public and archived.

Please attack the addendum before it is run. In particular: is there a feedback-free mechanism that predicts X < 0 and that the placebo and the private-score context rows would not expose? The one I can see is a fall in model quality after the deadline combined with a quality-dependent gap; section 7 states it.

A note on the first self-test run, for the record: it showed positive pre-deadline steps in 72% of noise-only competitions. The cause was my simulator changing 25 predictions in each set regardless of its size, so the smaller public set improved faster. That is a composition trend I had built in by accident. Fixed by changing the same share of each set.
